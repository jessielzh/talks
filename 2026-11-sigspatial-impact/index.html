In 2016, this paper was rejected.

Thank you. Three things, quickly — I know there is dessert.

Ten years ago, almost to the day, I got this email. The full paper was rejected. The meta-review said it in one word: rudimentary. Another reviewer: no novel contribution. In other words — too simple. They did let us in as a four-page short paper and a poster. So tonight, a poster is getting a 10-year impact award. (pause for laughter)

Clicks: (1) the email arrives, (2) the reviews, (3) the TOO SIMPLE stamp.

How long from home to work?

And they were right — it is simple. How long does it take from your home to your office? You answer right away. You don't add up road segments; you just know, because you've made that trip a thousand times.

The textbook way — the way we all teach — is: find the shortest path on the road graph, then add up the travel time edge by edge. We did something embarrassing. We threw the graph away. With 173 million New York taxi trips, someone has already driven from near your origin to near your destination. Just look at those trips and average them. That's it. And it beat the route-based state of the art and the online map services of the time.

2 / 4

What ten years taught us.

Three lessons. One: "data-driven" is easy to say. It really means trusting the data — enough to throw away the algorithm you were trained to love. Two: time will tell. A simple idea that is right doesn't need to look clever; give it ten years. Three: this community. The same SIGSPATIAL that called it rudimentary still let a poster in the door, and ten years later gave it this award. That's an open community.
